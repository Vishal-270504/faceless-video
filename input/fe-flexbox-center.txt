Centering a box used to take hours of guessing. Today it takes three lines of CSS.

Flexbox is a layout tool. You turn on flex on the parent, and its children line up for you.

Broken version: a box sits in the top left corner, held in place by margin tricks that break when the screen resizes.

The fix: set display flex on the parent. Then set justify-content to center, which centers on the horizontal axis.

Add align-items center, which centers on the vertical axis. The child now sits in the middle at any screen size.

Flex also spaces items in a row. Use gap, so you don't need margins on every child.

The takeaway: flex the parent, then align the children with two properties.
